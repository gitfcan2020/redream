PVR2 / OPAQUE PATH

The Tile Accelerator groups submitted primitives into 32 × 32 pixel tile lists.

Open the PowerVR2 engineering notes ↗

32 × 32 tiles. A 640 × 480 frame maps to 20 × 15, or 300 tile positions. Geometry can be binned for the tiles it overlaps.

Visibility first. For opaque geometry, hidden-surface removal runs before unnecessary texture fetches and shading. Transparent surfaces need different ordering.

Dedicated memory. The Dreamcast has 8 MB of video RAM, separate from its 16 MB main RAM and 2 MB audio RAM. Tile-local work reduces external framebuffer traffic.

Published peaks. Sega’s hardware outline lists 3 million polygons/s peak and 1.5 million visible polygons/s. Those are specification figures, not a real game’s frame budget.

A GENERATIONAL LEAP

Context beats
clock speed.

Four years separated the Saturn and Dreamcast. These are hardware specifications, not equivalent measures of game performance.

Saturn / 1994Dreamcast / 1998
Main CPU2 × SH-21 × SH-4
CPU clock28.6 MHz each200 MHz
Work RAM2 MB16 MB
GraphicsVDP1 + VDP2PowerVR2
MAIN MEMORY / SAME SCALE
Saturn2 MB
Dreamcast16 MB

7× the clock of one SH-2 and 8× the work RAM do not mean 7× or 8× the performance. Architecture, dual-CPU coordination, bandwidth and workload all matter. The SH-4’s published 1.4 GFLOPS is a theoretical peak, not a frame-rate guarantee.
